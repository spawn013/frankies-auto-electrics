The single biggest finding

Your custom.make metafield already exists, already holds 31 vehicle makes, and already powers a working make filter — on the Fascias and Harnesses collection. It is live on that one collection and nowhere else. Meanwhile In-Car Entertainment, your largest category at 6,441 products, ships filter CSS and JavaScript to every visitor and renders no filter controls at all. Someone did the hard part and stopped. That is not a rebuild — that is a rollout.

And the thing nobody in your market has done

I checked all seven competitors you named. Only one of them — Automotive Superstore — has a real vehicle fitment selector. Strathfield advertises "What Fits My Car" in their top navigation and the page returns No products found. JB Hi-Fi, Jaycar, Elite Car Audio and Bankstown Sound have nothing. Your product titles already carry the fitment data — "Toyota Hilux 2015-2020", "Honda CRV RE 2007-2011", "Prado 150 Series". It's sitting in text strings instead of in a filter.

02

What's already working — so nobody wastes money re-doing it

A good audit tells you where not to spend. Four things on this site are genuinely done well, and two of them are better than what most of your competitors have.

Product descriptions are not the problem

I sampled 3,000 products. Median description length is 1,409 characters and 69% run over 800. Only 1.5% are effectively empty. Whoever has been writing these has done real work. Don't let anyone sell you a "rewrite all your product copy" project — the gap is structured data, not prose.

Search is better than you'd expect

Searchanise is doing a good job. "hilux 2018 head unit", "prado 150 stereo" and "ranger px3 speakers" all return sensible, on-model results. This is the one area where you're ahead of Bankstown Sound and Elite Car Audio. It matches on text, not fitment, so it can serve the wrong generation — but the foundation is sound.

Category SEO has been started properly

Your top collections carry hand-written meta descriptions and FAQ schema — Fascias and Harnesses has 3,400 characters of category copy, Car Speakers has over 18,000. Product pages carry correct Product and BreadcrumbList structured data with SKU and brand. That's ahead of most stores this size.

You already built the booking system

The Code Safe page has a live appointment form capturing car_make, car_model, appointment_date and shop_location. That is a working vehicle-aware booking flow. It exists for immobilisers and Interlock — and nowhere for head units, speakers or dash cams, which is where the volume is.

03

The fitment gap is the whole opportunity

Everything else in this document is housekeeping. This is the one that changes the revenue line, and it's the one your competitors have left open.

A customer with a 2018 Hilux does not want to browse 6,441 in-car entertainment products. They want the four head units that fit their truck, the fascia that goes with it, the harness, and a price to have it fitted. Right now the site cannot answer that question, so they ring you — which the ad specifically says shouldn't have to happen — or they go to Automotive Superstore, who will answer it in two clicks with a rego lookup and a "Fitment guaranteed" badge.

The reason this is an opportunity rather than a problem is that the expensive half is already done. The compatibility data exists in three places: in your product titles, in the custom.make metafield, and in your "Vehicle Specific" navigation branches. What's missing is the wiring between them.

Asset 1 · The data is in your titles

Product names already encode make, model and year range: "Aerpro AMDTO44 10" Multimedia Receiver Kit Toyota Camry 2012-2017". Thousands of them. That is a parseable dataset, not a data-entry project.

Asset 2 · The metafield exists

custom.make is populated with 31 makes and renders as a working filter on Fascias and Harnesses. The field, the values and the theme code are all in place.

Asset 3 · The taxonomy anticipates it

Your menu already has Vehicle Specific head units, Vehicle Specific Speaker Spacers and Vehicle Specific Speaker Adaptors. The merchandising logic is there; the filtering isn't.

Asset 4 · The booking form works

Code Safe already captures make, model, date and location. Point that same pattern at car audio and you have installation booking — which none of the seven competitors offers properly.

The data needs cleaning before it's rolled out, though

The live make filter has errors visible from the front end: Mercedes and Mercedes Benz exist as two separate makes, Landrover is missing its space, and Falcon is listed as a make when it is a Ford model. Rolling this out across 750 collections before fixing the value list would multiply four errors into four hundred. That's an afternoon of work to prevent a month of cleanup.

Navigation, filtering & fitment

4 findings
NAV-01Critical

Your largest category has no filters at all

Evidence
In-Car Entertainment holds 6,441 products and renders zero filter controls. I confirmed this twice — in the raw HTML and on the rendered page. The theme still loads collection-filter.css and collection-filter.js on that page, so every visitor downloads a filter system that displays nothing.
Impact
A shopper landing on your biggest category can sort by price or scroll. That is the entire toolkit. This is the highest-traffic, highest-intent page on the site.
Fix
Enable the existing filter set on this collection. The code is already being served to the page.
FIT-01Critical

No vehicle fitment selector anywhere on the site

Evidence
No "shop by vehicle", no "select your vehicle", no year/make/model picker, no rego lookup on any page tested.
Impact
Every fitment question becomes a phone call, an email, or a lost sale. It also means you cannot compete for the searches that convert best — the ones with a vehicle in them.
Fix
Two-stage. Stage one is a make/model filter on the main collections using the metafield you already have. Stage two is a persistent vehicle selector. Automotive Superstore is the benchmark; nobody else in your set is close.
FIT-02Critical

The make filter you built is live on one collection out of 750

Evidence
filter.p.m.custom.make appears on Fascias and Harnesses with 31 make values. I tested twelve major collections — Car Speakers, Batteries, Lighting, 4WD and Caravan, Marine, Auto Electrical, Brake Parts and others — and none of them offer it.
Impact
You paid for this capability and are using less than 1% of it.
Fix
Clean the value list, backfill the metafield from product titles, then enable the filter across every vehicle-relevant collection.
NAV-02High

The product-type filter has 198 options, many of them duplicates

Evidence
On one collection the product_type facet offers 198 values including 9 Inch Fascia Kit with Integration Harness, 9-Inch Fascia Kit with Integration Harnesses, 9-Inch Installation Kit, 9-Inch Head Unit Installation Kit and 9/10-Inch Head Unit Installation Kit as separate options. Across the catalogue there are 367 distinct product types.
Impact
A filter with 198 near-identical options is worse than no filter — it makes the customer do the disambiguation work.
Fix
Collapse to a controlled vocabulary of roughly 60–80 types and enforce it on import. This is a mapping spreadsheet plus a bulk edit, not a rebuild.

Catalogue & product data

4 findings
CAT-01High

Two thirds of your products have exactly one image

Evidence
From a 3,000-product sample: 66.4% have a single image, average is 2.53, and only 29% have four or more.
Impact
Image count correlates directly with conversion on considered purchases. Jaycar runs 4–7 images per product; Bankstown Sound runs one, and it's their most obvious weakness.
Fix
Pull additional supplier images via feed for the top 500 products by revenue first. Don't touch the long tail.
CAT-02High

No structured specification table on product pages

Evidence
Specifications appear inside description prose. No spec table markup found on the product pages tested.
Impact
Two costs. Customers can't compare products, and you can't build technical filters — RMS power, DIN size, screen size, impedance — because the data isn't in fields. Jaycar publishes full Thiele-Small parameters plus mounting and cutout diameters; JB Hi-Fi publishes grouped spec tables on every head unit.
Fix
Metafield-driven spec table, populated for the top categories first. This unlocks CAT-03 and the technical filters in one move.
CAT-03High

One product in five has no product type assigned

Evidence
20.8% of sampled products have an empty product_type — roughly 3,600 products catalogue-wide.
Impact
Those products can't be filtered, can't be reliably merchandised into automated collections, and are hard to find unless you search their exact name.
Fix
Backfill against the cleaned vocabulary from NAV-02. Same project, run once.
CAT-04Medium

Heavy supplier concentration worth a commercial conversation

Evidence
Aerpro accounts for 911 of 3,000 sampled products — around 30% of the catalogue. 104 vendors in total.
Impact
Not a fault, but it's a margin and negotiation question I'd want the numbers behind. It also concentrates risk if terms or availability change.
Fix
Nothing on the website. This is a buying discussion, and I'd need cost data to have it properly.

SEO & content

7 findings
SEO-01Critical

Your homepage title is cut off mid-sentence

Evidence
The live title is Frankies Auto Electrics & Car Audio | Your One-Stop Shop for Car. It ends there. "for Car".
Impact
This is the first thing every customer sees in Google, in a shared link, and in a browser tab. It reads as unfinished.
Fix
Ten minutes. And it should carry the location — no competitor in your set owns "Wollongong" or "Illawarra" in their title tag.
SEO-02High

Two blog articles exist. Across three blogs.

Evidence
Three blogs are set up — Car Care, Product Guides, Gift Guides. Between them they contain two articles. Gift Guides is empty. The most recent car care post is from November 2025.
Impact
Autobarn has roughly 50 evergreen "Know How" articles. Strathfield publishes vehicle-specific install case studies about weekly. Bankstown Sound has ten, including a Hyundai i30 upgrade guide that functions as a fitment tool made of words. You are not in this race.
Fix
Vehicle-specific upgrade guides for the vehicles actually driven in the Illawarra — Hilux, Ranger, Prado, Triton, D-Max. Name the model years, which Bankstown Sound doesn't, and link each guide from the matching collection.
SEO-03High

Judge.me is installed but no star ratings reach Google

Evidence
Judge.me loads on product pages. The Product structured data contains no aggregateRating, and no review data surfaced on the products tested.
Impact
No stars in search results. Worth noting: every competitor I checked has the same problem — Jaycar and Bankstown Sound both have empty review widgets. Whoever fills theirs first gets the visual advantage in the results page.
Fix
Turn on post-purchase review requests and confirm the rating snippet is emitted. You have 30 years of customers to ask.
SEO-04Medium

Key pages have weak or missing metadata

Evidence
The contact page's title is just Contact with no meta description. The about page is titled Welcome To Frankies Auto Electrics. The Marine collection title runs 99 characters and will truncate in results; CB/UHF Radios and Accessories runs 29 with no brand name.
Impact
Your contact page is a local-search asset with a location, a phone number and 30 years of history behind it. Right now it's titled "Contact".
Fix
Template the patterns, then hand-write the top 50 collections. A day's work.
SEO-05Medium

The site contradicts itself on how long you've been trading

Evidence
The homepage says "Serving Illawarra for Over 30 Years". The about page headline says "Your Trusted Auto Electrical Experts for Over a Decade".
Impact
Small on its own, but it's the kind of thing a careful customer notices, and it undersells the strongest thing you have. Thirty years in the Illawarra is not a detail — it's the reason to buy from you instead of Automotive Superstore.
Fix
Pick the true number and use it consistently, including in the homepage title tag.
SEO-06Medium

The homepage has five H1s and no H2s

Evidence
Five <h1> elements, zero <h2>. The about page has five H1s of its own, the first claiming "Australia's top automotive marketplace".
Impact
Minor ranking effect, real accessibility effect — screen reader users navigate by heading level. Also, that claim sits oddly against a 30-year Illawarra story, which is the more credible and more defensible position.
Fix
Theme-level heading correction. One change, applies everywhere.
SEO-07Medium

261 collections hold fewer than ten products

Evidence
Of 750 collections: 261 have under 10 products, 150 have under 5, and 9 are empty.
Impact
Thin pages dilute crawl budget and give customers dead ends. Some are legitimately niche — "13-inch subwoofers" only has two products because only two exist — so this needs judgement, not a blanket delete.
Fix
Audit against search demand. Merge, redirect or noindex the ones with no traffic and no stock story.

Technical performance

4 findings
TEC-01Critical

The homepage ships a megabyte of HTML and 92 script tags

Evidence
Raw homepage HTML measures 1,023 KB before any assets load. It contains 92 script tags, 49 of them external, plus 121 KB of inline JavaScript. Server response time is fine — TTFB around 150 ms — so this is entirely front-end weight.
Impact
On my first cache-cold load the homepage took over 12 seconds to reach DOM-ready. A collection page came in around 3.6 seconds. Treat these as indicative rather than lab-grade — I'd confirm against your real Core Web Vitals — but the megabyte of HTML and the 92 scripts are facts, not measurements, and both point the same way.
Fix
Audit the app stack, defer what isn't critical, and cut the inline JavaScript. This is where my background is directly useful.
TEC-02Medium

You're paying two vendors to record the same sessions

Evidence
Microsoft Clarity and FullStory both load on every page, alongside GTM, the Meta pixel, Searchanise, Judge.me and Ahrefs analytics.
Impact
Two session-recording tools doing the same job, both costing page weight, and I'd guess only one is actually being watched.
Fix
Pick one, remove the other. Clarity is free and adequate for most of what you'd want.
TEC-03Medium

Filter code is served to pages that display no filters

Evidence
collection-filter.css and collection-filter.js load on In-Car Entertainment, which renders no filter UI.
Impact
Small in bytes, but it's a reliable signal that the filter rollout was started and abandoned partway. Worth knowing before anyone quotes you for "building filtering".
Fix
Resolved automatically by fixing NAV-01.
TEC-04Medium

Nearly half the homepage images have no alt text

Evidence
35 of 80 images on the homepage have no alt attribute or an empty one.
Impact
Accessibility obligation, lost image search traffic, and for a business selling on visual product recognition, Google Images is not nothing.
Fix
Template alt text from product titles, hand-write the banners.

06

What I'd do in the first 90 days

Sequenced so the cheap, high-certainty work funds the credibility for the bigger pieces. I'd expect to revise all of this in week one once I can see the actual numbers.

Days 1–30

Measure, then fix what's already broken

No new projects. Establish the baseline and clear the errors that cost nothing to fix.

  • Get the numbers. Revenue, margin, conversion rate, AOV, ad spend and ROAS by channel, top 100 products by revenue and by margin. Everything after this depends on it.
  • Fix SEO-01 in week one. The truncated homepage title. Then the contact and about page metadata.
  • Turn the filters on for In-Car Entertainment. The code is already loading on the page.
  • Clean the make list — merge Mercedes, fix Landrover, remove Falcon — before it gets rolled out anywhere else.
  • Audit the app stack. Drop one of the two session recorders, defer non-critical scripts, and get a real Core Web Vitals baseline.
  • Switch on review requests so Judge.me starts collecting against 30 years of customers.
How you'd judge itBaseline dashboard exists and is agreed. Homepage title fixed. Largest category filterable. Page weight down measurably.
Days 31–60

Roll out fitment

The one piece of work that changes the shape of the business rather than tidying it.

  • Backfill custom.make and add model and year by parsing the fitment data already sitting in product titles. Machine-assisted, human-checked on a sample before it goes live.
  • Enable make/model filtering across every vehicle-relevant collection, not just Fascias.
  • Collapse the 367 product types to a controlled list and backfill the 20% that have none.
  • Build vehicle landing pages for the top 10 Illawarra vehicles — Hilux, Ranger, Prado, D-Max, Triton and so on — each one a real page with the products that fit and a fitting price.
  • Add the Downloads block to product pages, starting with head units.
How you'd judge itMake filter live sitewide. Ten vehicle pages ranking and converting. Organic entries on vehicle-model queries starting to appear in Search Console.
Days 61–90

Turn the workshop into an online product

Now use the thing none of the seven competitors can copy.

  • Clone the Code Safe booking flow for car audio fitting, with indicative pricing so people aren't ringing to ask.
  • Sell supply-and-fit as a single SKU for the top vehicles — the Strathfield pattern, executed better because you actually run the workshop.
  • Publish vehicle upgrade guides on a fixed cadence, each linked from its collection and each naming the model years.
  • Spec tables on the top categories, which then unlock technical filters — RMS power, DIN size, screen size.
  • Reset the advertising against the new landing pages, reported on spend, sales and margin rather than impressions.
How you'd judge itInstallation bookings arriving through the site. Supply-and-fit AOV vs product-only AOV. Ad spend traceable to gross profit, not clicks.
07

What I'd need — and what I'd want help with

The job ad covers eight disciplines. I'm strong in four of them and I'd rather say so now than discover it in month six.

Where I'm strongest

Technical and data. Site performance, Shopify build work, technical SEO, structured data, filtering and metafield architecture, conversion diagnosis, and reading analytics to work out where the money is leaking. Everything in this document came out of that skill set — and it's the foundation the rest of the list sits on.

Where I'd want support

Paid campaign strategy, ad creative, and commercial pricing calls need industry judgement rather than technical skill. AI tooling closes a lot of that gap now — drafting, campaign structure, first-pass competitor and pricing analysis — but I'd still want someone with real automotive marketing experience in the loop, even part-time, to sanity-check positioning. I'd rather build that in from day one than pretend it isn't needed.

Access I'd need

  • Shopify admin and theme access
  • GA4, Search Console, Merchant Center
  • Google Ads and Meta accounts
  • Cost and margin data by product
  • Supplier feeds and how product data currently arrives

Decisions I'd need from you

  • Can I change prices, and up to what threshold?
  • Can I reallocate ad spend within an agreed budget?
  • Is there budget for freelance help — dev (theo's team is available so can be skipped), copy, photography, data entry?
  • What are the top three priorities if not everything can happen at once?

One honest note on scale

17,491 products is a lot of catalogue for one person. Checking part number, description, specs, images, application and category on every product at ten minutes each is roughly 2,900 hours — well over a year of full-time work doing nothing else. That's why the plan above fixes the systems that make the catalogue better at scale, and only hand-works the top few hundred products by revenue. Anyone who tells you they'll manually perfect all 17,491 either hasn't done the arithmetic or isn't planning to, but if the product managers and/or product listers are still available for help then, this will be achievable.

08

Method, and what I couldn't see

How this was produced

Everything here was measured from public pages on 31 August 2026 — no admin access, no analytics, no conversations with anyone at Frankie's. Catalogue statistics come from a 3,000-product sample drawn evenly across the full catalogue. Filter, metadata, structured data and page-weight figures were read directly from the live HTML. Competitor findings come from their live sites.

What I'd want to check before anyone acts on this

  • Performance figures are indicative, not lab-grade. They're single measurements from one browser on one connection. The megabyte of HTML and the 92 script tags are hard facts; the load times need confirming against your real Core Web Vitals data.
  • Autobarn blocks automated access. Their content library and installation warranty are confirmed from pages that did load. Platform, fitment selector and pricing are inferred from a sibling site and are flagged as unverified throughout.
  • "Bankstown" was interpreted as Bankstown Sound on Marion Street — the only car audio business in that suburb with a substantial transactional site. If you meant a different shop, that section is about the wrong business and I'd redo it.
  • Sampling is sampling. The 66% single-image and 20.8% missing-product-type figures come from 3,000 products, not all 17,491. I'd expect them to hold, but I'd verify against the full export before building a plan on them.
  • I can't see the commercial picture at all. No revenue, margin, traffic, conversion or ad performance data. Some of what looks like a problem from outside may already be understood, deliberate, or not worth fixing. That's the first conversation, not an assumption I'd make.
